I work on AI safety and security. My interest is in the gap between a system that performs well under evaluation and one that holds up when the world stops cooperating: text placed on a physical object that redirects a manipulation robot, a perturbation that breaks the moment a model decides which object an instruction refers to, advice that is technically correct and useless to the person who has to live with it. In each case the failure is not noise. It is a property of how the system was built and what its evaluation was never designed to notice.

Right now that means adversarial attacks on vision language action models with Yuan Tian at UCLA, typographic attacks on household manipulation robots with Peng Liu at Penn State, and LLM safety evaluation with Liwei Jiang.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027. If you work on robustness of embodied systems, multimodal attacks, or safety evaluation, I would like to hear from you.

HearSayBench: Can LLMs Navigate from Abstract Human Rights to Lived Lives?

Ali Iranmanesh*, Ava Iranmanesh*, Sobhan Lotfi*, Liwei Jiang

A benchmark probing whether models recognize the unstated structural constraints that make standard advice impossible for people living under adversarial conditions. Across 11 frontier and open weight models we find a systemic 37% drop between understanding a situation and giving advice that respects it. Oral at the Pluralistic Alignment Workshop, ICML 2026.

Not What You Asked For: Typographic Attacks in Household Robot Manipulation

Ali Iranmanesh, Peng Liu

Text placed on ordinary objects in a room can override the instruction a manipulation robot was given, without touching the model, the prompt, or the network. We characterize which placements transfer across scenes and why the vision language backbone treats printed text as instruction rather than as scenery.
